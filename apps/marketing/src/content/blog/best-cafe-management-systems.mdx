Running a small cafe means wearing many hats — barista, manager, accountant, and scheduler, often all at once. The right management system can take several of those hats off your head. But with dozens of options on the market, how do you choose?

We compared 8 popular cafe management systems based on what actually matters to small cafe owners: ease of use, pricing, and the features you need from day one.

## What to look for in a cafe management system

Before diving into the list, here's what matters most for small cafes:

- **Affordable pricing** — You're not a chain. You shouldn't pay like one.
- **POS + ordering in one place** — Separate systems mean separate headaches.
- **No hardware lock-in** — Use your own tablet or laptop.
- **Quick setup** — You don't have weeks to configure software.
- **Inventory basics** — Know when you're running low on milk before you run out.

## The 8 best cafe management systems in 2026

### 1. Gustri

**Best for:** Small cafes that want everything in one platform without the enterprise price tag.

Gustri is an all-in-one restaurant management platform that includes POS, QR ordering, kitchen display, delivery management, inventory tracking, staff scheduling, and analytics — all built in from the start.

**What stands out:**
- Generous free plan (1 terminal, 50 menu items, 5 tables, QR ordering included)
- Works on any device — no special hardware needed
- 20 languages supported out of the box
- Kitchen display system included, not an add-on
- Real-time order updates between front-of-house and kitchen

**Pricing:** Free plan available. Paid plans from $29/mo.

**Pros:**
- Everything included — no plugins or add-ons to buy
- Clean, modern interface that staff learn in minutes
- QR ordering lets customers order from their phone
- Split payments (by amount, percentage, or items)

**Cons:**
- Newer platform, smaller community compared to legacy systems
- No offline mode yet (requires internet connection)

---

### 2. Square for Restaurants

**Best for:** Cafes that already use Square for payments.

Square's restaurant-specific POS is solid and well-known. The free plan covers basic POS needs, and the hardware ecosystem is polished.

**Pricing:** Free plan available. Plus plan from $60/mo.

**Pros:**
- Reliable payment processing
- Good hardware options
- Strong reporting

**Cons:**
- QR ordering and kitchen display are paid add-ons
- Free plan is limited — most useful features require Plus ($60/mo)
- Locked into Square payment processing

---

### 3. Toast

**Best for:** US-based restaurants that want a full hardware + software package.

Toast is a popular choice in the US market with purpose-built hardware. It's feature-rich but comes with a higher price point and commitment.

**Pricing:** Free POS plan available. Essentials from $69/mo.

**Pros:**
- Purpose-built restaurant hardware
- Strong online ordering features
- Large US support network

**Cons:**
- Hardware is mandatory (can't use your own tablet)
- Higher total cost when you factor in hardware fees
- US-only
- Long-term contracts on most plans

---

### 4. Lightspeed Restaurant

**Best for:** Mid-size cafes and restaurants that want deep analytics.

Lightspeed offers a mature restaurant POS with strong reporting and integrations. It's more complex but powerful for growing operations.

**Pricing:** From $69/mo (billed annually).

**Pros:**
- Advanced reporting and analytics
- Large integration marketplace
- Good multi-location support

**Cons:**
- No free plan
- Steeper learning curve
- Can get expensive with add-ons
- Annual billing required for best pricing

---

### 5. iiko

**Best for:** Restaurants in CIS/Eastern European markets.

iiko is a well-known restaurant management system popular in Russia, Azerbaijan, and neighboring countries. It's feature-rich but comes with complexity.

**Pricing:** Subscription-based, pricing varies by region. Typically $50-100+/mo.

**Pros:**
- Deep feature set for full-service restaurants
- Popular in CIS markets with local support
- Strong inventory and cost control features

**Cons:**
- Complex setup — often needs a consultant
- Interface feels dated compared to modern alternatives
- Pricing isn't transparent (requires contacting sales)
- Overkill for small cafes

---

### 6. SumUp

**Best for:** Very small cafes and food trucks that just need payments + basic POS.

SumUp is primarily a payment processor that added POS features. It's simple and affordable but limited in restaurant-specific functionality.

**Pricing:** Free POS app. Card reader from $39 (one-time).

**Pros:**
- Very low cost to start
- Simple card reader hardware
- No monthly fees on basic plan

**Cons:**
- Limited restaurant features (no kitchen display, basic inventory)
- No QR ordering
- Per-transaction fees add up at higher volumes
- Not designed specifically for restaurants

---

### 7. Poster POS

**Best for:** Small restaurants in Eastern Europe and CIS markets.

Poster is a cloud-based POS system with a clean interface. It covers the basics well at a reasonable price point.

**Pricing:** From $33/mo.

**Pros:**
- Clean, modern interface
- Good multi-language support
- Reasonable pricing

**Cons:**
- No free plan
- Limited integrations compared to larger platforms
- Kitchen display is basic
- No built-in delivery management

---

### 8. Loyverse

**Best for:** Very small cafes that need a free POS with basic features.

Loyverse offers a free POS app that works on tablets. It's simple and covers the basics, but restaurant-specific features are limited.

**Pricing:** Free POS. Add-ons from $5/mo each.

**Pros:**
- Free core POS
- Works on iPad and Android
- Simple loyalty program included

**Cons:**
- Add-ons required for most useful features (inventory, employee management)
- No QR ordering
- No kitchen display system
- Limited to basic cafe operations

## Our recommendation

If you're a small cafe looking for the most value, **Gustri** gives you the most complete feature set at the lowest price point — especially with the free plan that includes QR ordering and kitchen display.

For cafes already invested in the Square ecosystem, **Square for Restaurants** is a solid choice but budget for the Plus plan to get meaningful features.

For the absolute simplest setup, **Loyverse** or **SumUp** get you started quickly, but you'll likely outgrow them fast.

The key is to pick a system that won't require piecing together multiple tools. Every additional integration is another potential point of failure — and another monthly bill.
