Food cost is the single biggest expense for most restaurants — typically 28-35% of revenue. The difference between a profitable restaurant and one that's struggling often comes down to how well they manage inventory.

This guide covers everything you need to know about restaurant inventory management — from basic stock tracking to reducing waste and controlling costs. No theory, just practical advice that works for small cafes and restaurants.

## Why inventory management matters

Let's start with numbers. For a cafe doing $30,000/month in revenue:

- At 35% food cost: **$10,500/month** on ingredients
- At 30% food cost: **$9,000/month** on ingredients
- **Difference: $1,500/month — $18,000/year**

That 5% reduction is achievable with basic inventory management. Most small cafes that start tracking inventory find 5-10% savings within the first few months. That's money going straight to your bottom line.

## The basics: what to track

### Stock levels

Know what you have. This seems obvious, but most small cafes run on guesswork — someone peeks in the fridge and decides whether to order more milk.

**What to track:**
- Current quantity of each ingredient
- Minimum stock level (the point at which you need to reorder)
- Unit of measurement (kg, liters, pieces)

**How often:** Count your key items daily (proteins, dairy, bread). Count everything else weekly.

### Stock movements

Track what comes in and what goes out:

- **In:** Deliveries from suppliers (date, quantity, cost)
- **Out:** Used in orders (automatic if your POS tracks this), manual adjustments, waste

### Waste

This is where most restaurants lose money without realizing it. Track every item that gets thrown away:

- **Spoilage** — food that expired or went bad
- **Overproduction** — made too much, nobody ordered it
- **Mistakes** — wrong orders, dropped plates
- **Theft** — it happens, and tracking makes it visible

## Setting up inventory management

### Step 1: Create your inventory list

List every ingredient and supply item you use. For a typical small cafe, this is 50-150 items. Group them by category:

- **Proteins** — chicken, beef, fish, eggs
- **Dairy** — milk, cream, cheese, butter
- **Produce** — vegetables, fruits, herbs
- **Dry goods** — flour, sugar, rice, pasta, coffee beans
- **Beverages** — juices, soda, beer, wine
- **Supplies** — takeout containers, napkins, cleaning products

### Step 2: Set par levels

Par level = the minimum quantity you should have on hand. When stock drops below this level, it's time to reorder.

**How to calculate par level:**
1. Daily usage: How much do you use per day on average?
2. Lead time: How long does it take to get a delivery?
3. Safety buffer: Add 20-30% for unexpected busy days

**Example:** You use 5 liters of milk daily. Supplier delivers in 2 days. Par level = (5 x 2) x 1.25 = **12.5 liters**.

### Step 3: Count and record

Do a full inventory count to establish your baseline. Then maintain it:

- **Daily:** Count high-value and high-turnover items (proteins, dairy, coffee)
- **Weekly:** Count everything else
- **Monthly:** Full detailed count with cost calculations

### Step 4: Track waste daily

Create a simple waste log:

| Date | Item | Quantity | Reason | Cost |
|------|------|----------|--------|------|
| Mar 5 | Croissants | 4 | Unsold (end of day) | $6 |
| Mar 5 | Milk | 0.5L | Expired | $1 |
| Mar 5 | Chicken wrap | 1 | Wrong order | $4 |

After a month, you'll see patterns: always throwing away croissants on Tuesdays? Make fewer on Tuesdays. Milk expiring? Order less or more frequently.

## Reducing food waste

Food waste is the easiest win. Most restaurants waste 5-10% of their food purchases. Here's how to cut that down:

### 1. FIFO — First In, First Out

The golden rule: use older stock before newer stock. When a delivery arrives, put new items behind old ones. Label everything with the date received.

This alone can reduce spoilage by 30-50%.

### 2. Prep smarter

Don't prep for your best day — prep for your average day. It's better to run out of pre-prepped salad and make a fresh one than to throw away salad at closing.

**Tips:**
- Prep in smaller batches, more frequently
- Track which prep items get wasted most — adjust quantities
- Use a prep sheet based on reservation count and historical sales

### 3. Cross-utilize ingredients

Design your menu so that ingredients are used in multiple dishes. If only one dish uses avocado and it doesn't sell well, you're throwing away avocados.

**Example:** Chicken breast used in chicken wrap, chicken salad, and chicken soup. If one dish is slow, the ingredient still moves through the others.

### 4. Track and review waste weekly

Spend 15 minutes every Monday reviewing last week's waste log:

- What was wasted most?
- Why?
- What can you change this week?

Small weekly adjustments compound into significant savings over months.

### 5. Use your data to adjust ordering

After 4-6 weeks of tracking, you'll have enough data to optimize your ordering:

- **Slow days:** Order less before historically slow days
- **Seasonal shifts:** Adjust for weather and seasons
- **Menu changes:** When you add or remove items, adjust ingredient orders immediately

## Common inventory mistakes

### 1. Not tracking at all

"I know what we have" — no, you don't. Even experienced chefs underestimate waste and overestimate stock. Track it.

### 2. Counting too infrequently

Monthly inventory counts catch problems too late. By the time you realize you've been wasting 10% of your chicken, you've already lost hundreds of dollars. Count high-value items daily.

### 3. Ignoring waste

Throwing away food feels normal in a restaurant. It shouldn't. Every thrown-away item is money in the trash. Track it, review it, reduce it.

### 4. Over-ordering "just in case"

Buying extra "to be safe" leads to spoilage. It's better to have a system that alerts you at the right time than to stockpile perishables.

### 5. Not adjusting for sales trends

Your menu mix changes with seasons, weather, day of week, and local events. Your ordering should reflect these patterns, not stay static.

## A simple weekly inventory routine

Here's a practical routine that takes 30 minutes per week:

**Daily (5 minutes):**
- Count key items (proteins, dairy, bread, coffee)
- Log any waste
- Check for items below par level

**Weekly (Monday morning, 25 minutes):**
- Full inventory count
- Review waste log from last week
- Adjust orders for the coming week
- Note any items consistently over or under-stocked

**Monthly (1 hour):**
- Full detailed count with cost calculations
- Calculate actual food cost percentage
- Compare to target (usually 28-32%)
- Review supplier prices — are you getting the best deal?
- Identify top 5 wasted items and create action plans
