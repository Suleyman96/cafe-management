"Free" is a loaded word in restaurant software. Some systems are genuinely free. Others are free to start but charge for every feature you actually need. And some use "free" to get you in the door before locking you into expensive hardware or processing fees.

This guide breaks down what's really free, what's not, and which free POS systems are actually worth using for your restaurant or cafe.

## What "free POS" actually means

There are three types of "free" in the POS world:

1. **Genuinely free tier** — A usable product at no cost, with paid upgrades for more features or scale. This is the best model for small restaurants.

2. **Free software, paid hardware** — The app is free but you must buy proprietary hardware (often $500-2,000+). Your "free" POS just cost you a lot.

3. **Free software, high transaction fees** — No monthly fee, but every card payment takes a larger cut. At volume, this costs more than a monthly subscription.

Knowing which model you're dealing with saves you from surprises.

## Hidden costs of "free" POS systems

Before choosing a free POS, calculate the real cost:

### Transaction fee math

| Monthly card sales | 2.6% + 10¢ (Square) | 2.75% (SumUp) | 2.99% + 15¢ (Toast) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $5,000/mo | $140 | $138 | $157 |
| $10,000/mo | $270 | $275 | $314 |
| $20,000/mo | $530 | $550 | $613 |

At $10,000/mo in card sales, the transaction fee difference between Square and Toast is $44/mo. Over a year, that's $528 — enough to pay for a full-featured POS subscription.

### Add-on cost math

A "free" POS with $5/employee + $25 inventory + $9 integrations:
- Solo cafe: $39/mo in add-ons
- 3 employees: $49/mo in add-ons
- 5 employees: $59/mo in add-ons

At that point, you're paying more than an all-in-one system that includes everything.

## What to actually look for in a free POS
